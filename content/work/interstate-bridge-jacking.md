## The work

Bearings wear out long before the bridges that sit on them. Replacing one means lifting the superstructure off its supports, a small distance and under tight control, without overstressing anything.

I engineered cost-effective jacking and temporary support designs for about 15 interstate steel bridges along I-76 and I-476 in Greater Philadelphia, enabling bearing repair and replacement while minimizing the use of temporary towers.

## Why it is hard

A jacking design moves the load path of an existing bridge to a place it was never designed to use. Each one has to answer the same questions:

- Where can a jack push, and can the girder and the pier carry the load at that point?
- What stiffening or temporary support is needed, and how little of it will do?
- How far can one support be lifted before the neighboring members are overstressed?

Temporary towers answer these questions easily, and expensively. The cost-effective answer is usually to jack from the structure that is already there, and that is what these designs aimed for.

## Leading the task

I was also Structures Task Leader for the Jupiter bridge jacking projects on MLK (2023 – 2024). I managed design and plan development and controlled deliverables, schedule and budget, delivering on time in a construction-services setting, which runs differently from a conventional DOT design project. The pace is set by the construction schedule.

## Repairs

- Damage assessment, repair design and construction inspection for damaged girders, floorbeams and connections on a fracture-critical box girder bridge.
- Repair of truck-impact damage on an I-76 bridge: a punctured web and a rotated bottom flange.

## What it shows

Rehabilitation is engineering with the constraints already built. The structure exists, it is in service, and the answer has to fit what is there. It rewards the engineer who checks the actual condition before trusting the drawing.
