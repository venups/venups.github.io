## The pattern

When a calculation has to be done the same way a third time, I write the tool. That has been true in every role since my first one, and it is the thread that connects the bridge work to the software work.

## Selected tools

**Wind-load automation for CSiBridge (GFT).** The US 20 over Fox River models needed a large number of user-defined wind loads. I automated their input and cut the manual time by more than 80%.

**Connection design from STAAD.Pro (GFT).** For the River Street Bridge over Amtrak in Boston, an OpenSTAAD and Excel VBA process extracts forces from multiple STAAD.Pro bridge models and produces automated connection designs and calculation reports. I later presented the workflow to the bridge group as a quality-improvement session.

**Shop-drawing review tool (GFT).** An Excel tool for mast arm shop drawings that processes numerous measurements and streamlines checks of bolt spacing, edge distances and related geometry.

**Erection analysis pipeline (Modjeski and Masters).** Excel VBA macros integrated with SAP2000 to model, load and check the curved arch girders of the Robert L.B. Tobin Land Bridge for strength and stability through its construction sequence.

**Web-based design programs (Tata Consulting Engineers).** Customized programs in C# and ASP.NET that automated calculations such as staircase and retaining wall design. I led and mentored a team of five young engineers on the effort.

**Documentation from analysis output (Tata Consulting Engineers).** OpenSTAAD and Excel VBA to turn STAAD.Pro output into project documentation.

## What it shows

None of these started as a software project. Each started as a deadline. The habit of turning a one-off fix into something reusable is what later became Steel Bridge Pal and the GFT Transportation suite.
