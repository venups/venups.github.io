## The project

The Newark Bay–Hudson County Extension program includes the Newark Bay Bridge Replacement. The work combines model-based delivery with construction-stage analysis of steel I-girder bridges, on a scale where consistency between engineers matters as much as any single calculation.

## Why consistency is the hard part

One bridge can be carried by one careful engineer. Ten bridges cannot. They are modeled by different people, reviewed by different people and revised at different times. If each engineer sets up a model in a personal way, every review starts with working out how this one was built. My job was to make the ten behave like one body of work.

## My role

**Model-based delivery.** I served as BIM lead, organizing Bentley Blueprint sessions and a shared page and channel of OpenBridge Modeler training documents and recorded videos, so the team learned one way of modeling.

**Construction-stage analysis.** I led the finite element discussions on the questions that decide how steel I-girders are built: staged deflection, common-bay cross-frame installation, architectural camber, haunches, longitudinal live-load force and girder staging.

**Review.** I set up the Bluebeam drawing-review procedures so reviews run the same way across the bridge team.

**People.** I mentored junior engineers, and gave them ownership of templates and specialized calculations.

## The tool it produced

Rebuilding each bridge in CSiBridge from the OpenBridge model would have repeated the same expert work ten times. That is the problem [Steel Bridge Pal](/work/steel-bridge-pal) solves, saving an estimated 200 hours per model and about 2,000 hours across the program.

## What it shows

On a large program the lead's real product is the way of working: the training, the templates, the review procedure and the tools. The same four things decide whether a software product is adopted.
