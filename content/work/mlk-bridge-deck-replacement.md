## The project

The deck replacement of the MLK Jr. Bridge in Philadelphia covered a structure about 700 ft long, heavily skewed and fracture critical, at a historically significant location, with extensive superstructure and substructure repairs.

## Why it is hard

- **Fracture critical** means the bridge has steel tension members with no backup. If one fails, part of the bridge can come down. Every detail that touches those members is held to a higher standard.
- **A heavy skew** means nothing meets at a right angle. Deck reinforcement, joints and framing all need geometry worked out case by case.
- **A historic site** limits what can be changed and brings more reviewers to the table.
- **It is a rehabilitation.** The new deck has to fit steel that already exists, in the condition it is actually in.

## My role

I served as assistant project manager and lead structural engineer on the final design PS&E package: the plans, specifications and estimate a contractor bids on and builds from.

- Personally developed about 50 of the 92 structural contract drawings, along with specifications and the estimate.
- Coordinated with the client, a sub-consultant team and an in-house team of six engineers.
- Took part in decisions on approach roadway design, lighting and drainage, and supported public involvement.

## What it shows

A complete PS&E package is a product with many stakeholders and no room for a missing detail. I carried this one from the calculations to the drawings to the coordination.
